One man built a three-point-seven trillion dollar chip empire, and lost it in five days.
For thirty-three years, Jensen Huang ran Nvidia with sixty direct reports, no heir apparent, and only three-point-five percent of the shares.
Then came November eighth. Reports linked him to an extramarital affair with an executive at Megaspeed, a firm under investigation for smuggling Nvidia AI chips to China. Congress demanded answers. The SEC opened an inquiry. And Nvidia's board launched an independent probe.
Four days later, on November twelfth, Jensen Huang resigned on the spot.
Luckily, Elon Musk, Donald Trump, and a petition rallied to bring him back. Unluckily, the board he never gave supervoting rights to said no.
Think of it like building the world's fastest race car, but handing the spare keys to the pit crew. When the engine overheats, the driver doesn't get to overrule the mechanics.
Interim CEO Colette Kress took the wheel, and the board hired headhunters to replace its own founder permanently.
Even worse for Jensen: Nvidia's first earnings call without him just beat Wall Street's targets anyway.
